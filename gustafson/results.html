Scaled speed-up and the Gustafson fit

The scaled speed-up S = τ(P) / T(P) compares the serial program on the scaled problem of N(P) stars with the MPI program on the same stars and P ranks, both per time step. Points are medians, bars run from the least to the most favourable pairing of repeats. A fixed serial part, or a cost that grows with P?

s(P) = (P − S) / (P − 1) is the serial fraction that each P implies on its own, the weak-scaling counterpart of the Karp–Flatt metric. If it stays flat, a fixed serial part per step explains the curve, which is what Gustafson's law assumes. If it rises with P, a cost grows with the number of ranks: the Allgather of all positions, or waiting for the slowest rank. Where the extra time per step goes

Each bar is the extra time per step of the scaled problem on P ranks against the base problem on one core, T(P) − T(1), in % of T(1). It adds up to 1/E − 1. Compute is the mean rank's force and integration time: with N(P) chosen to keep the serial time per rank, it stays near zero when the cost model holds. Waiting covers everything else in the step loop: waiting for the slowest rank, the stop check and Python's own work per step.

How fast the problem has to grow

One step of direct summation costs a·N + b·N² on one core: a per star (NumPy's work per row of the force loop) and b per pair. For v1 the Amdahl campaign gives a = — µs and b = — ns. Keeping the pairs per rank fixed, N = N0·√P, hands every rank fewer but longer rows than the base problem, and those cost less time per pair. The serial work then grows by less than P, and the weak efficiency can pass 100 % without any gain from the parallelisation. This campaign therefore picks N(P) so that τ(N(P)) = P·τ(N0) (solid). The dashed sweeps use N0·√P for comparison: at P = 24 the serial work of N0 = 100 grows — times with the time rule and — times with N0·√P.

How the numbers were made, and what they leave out
  • gustafson/run_all.sh submitted one LSF job per sweep. Each job reserved a whole node and, for every P = 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16 and 24, ran the MPI program on N(P) stars with P ranks and the serial program on the same N(P) stars, back to back, three times, with merging off, one thread per rank and ranks bound to cores, socket 0 first (as in the Amdahl campaign).
  • N(P) comes from gustafson/plan.py: with the rule time it solves a·N + b·N² = P·(a·N0 + b·N0²), with the rule pairs it is N0·√P, both rounded to whole stars. The subsets are the nearest-N selections of GaiaSubsetSelection.py, nested like the earlier ones.
  • Step loop: simulation_time_s / iterations, the time steps on the slowest rank. Program: total_time_s. Whole launch: the wall time of each launch, measured by the job script. The program and launch measures compare whole runs and only exist for E.
  • The numbers are embedded in this file by gustafson/make_page.py from the outputs of gustafson/gustafson_analysis.py (and, for the comparison with fixed N, amdahl/amdahl_analysis.py).
  • Not covered: more than one node (the Allgather over InfiniBand), N0 above 1,000 (the serial runs of the scaled problem get long: N0 = 5,000 needs N(24) ≈ 25,000 stars and about 70 s per serial step, some 45 minutes per repeat), other parallelisations, and the lower clock frequency when all 24 cores are busy, which is not separated from the other losses.
