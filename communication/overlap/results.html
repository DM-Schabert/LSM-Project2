Why the ring's overlap does not help

The ring can start sending the next block (Isend/Irecv) and compute while it travels. In C4 that brought nothing, on one node and on two, and at small N it cost 7–10 %. Here two ranks exchange one message of each size while they compute with the force kernel, to see whether the transfer is hidden at all, and why not.

Hypothesis. MPI moves a message only while the program is inside an MPI call; nothing runs in the background. A small message (below the eager limit) is copied out at Isend and has arrived by the time the computation ends. A large one (rendezvous protocol) first needs a handshake between the two ranks, which only happens inside an MPI call, so the whole transfer starts at Waitall, after the computation: nothing is hidden. Calling Testall between the kernel tiles drives the transfer on and should hide it after all. And in our runs the transfers are so short against the computation that overlap could not have shown even if it had worked.

How much of the transfer is hidden

Share of the transfer that is hidden behind the kernel: 1 − (time in Waitall) / Tcomm, where Tcomm is the same exchange with Sendrecv and no computation. At 1 the message has arrived when the computation ends; at 0 the whole transfer happens in Waitall. Dashed: the message size of the ring at N = 5 000 and 20 000, P = 48. The x axis is logarithmic.

What overlap could have saved in our runs

Every run of the ring in C2 and C3. A message carries ⌈N/P⌉ positions of 24 bytes; per step there are P − 1 of them. The most overlap could ever save is all of their transfer time, (P − 1) · Tcomm, measured above (one node: same-node pair; two nodes: the pair across nodes, because the slowest hop sets the pace). Against that: what overlap actually changed in C4.

Every measured point
How it is measured
  • Two ranks, each sends m bytes to the other and receives m bytes, as two neighbours of the ring do in every round. Same node: --map-by core (cores 0 and 1); two nodes: --map-by ppr:1:node. Two full nodes are reserved, so no other job shares them.
  • The computation is the real force kernel in tiles of 2 × 1 000 pairs (they fit into the L2 cache), so many tiles take about twice as long as the transfer, at least 4 tiles. Polling: one Testall after every tile until the requests are done.
  • Every iteration starts after a barrier; the slower of the two ranks counts. Per size and mode the median of 30 iterations; the sizes run three times (the second time in reverse order), and the page shows the median over the three.
  • The job log lists the transport Open MPI uses (UCX or ob1) and its eager and rendezvous limits, to compare with the measured switch.
